1. Foundational & global

The app should never feel like a different product from WVDUNA.com. It inherits the same design system (assets/colors_and_type.css and assets/styles.css): deep navy ground, Old Gold accent, Goudy display + Avenir body, the ridge motif. The new app-specific layer lives in assets/app.css and assets/app.js.

3. The new visitor site

The logged-out site needs a rebuild so it mirrors the logged-in structure. The current per-audience pages (DUNAs, Members, Founders, Builders, Sponsors, Catalysts, Luminaries) collapse into one searchable, sortable Directory of cards: DUNAs, Members, Alliances, Programs. Each card links to that entity's Ally (which opens a chat). The same Directory exists logged-in (§16); logged-out it is the public face.

4. Onboarding → first purchase handoff

Account creation and onboarding stay on the web (§9). The moment a first-time user finishes onboarding, route them to the Set your role screen (§7) to buy their initial Coins. Their starting role and pricing are determined by the Kinship Code they entered at registration. If they entered none — or entered WVD-WVD-WVD — apply the WV DUNA Code, which mirrors the public home-page pricing exactly.

The button between the profile avatar and the notifications bell (currently a shortcut to Earn) should instead open an account / role management page. When already logged in, this is not a Stripe card checkout; it is Load Wallet.

9. iOS / Android reader-app handling

Per Apple/Google rules, the installed mobile apps are reader apps: they cannot create accounts or sell Coins in-app. The prototype assumes web, but this must be handled at build time.

10. Active Mode

Active Mode keeps its five sections from the published end-user app: Chat, Vibe, Seek, Vote, Earn. Changes are mostly vocabulary, theming, and wiring Coins.

11. Allies (was Agents & Allies)

New page copy: "Allies are intelligent agents that represent people, organizations, programs, and alliances. They communicate, coordinate, and act on behalf of their principals, carrying the identity, authority, and permissions needed to operate safely across the network."

Five types

  • DUNA Allies — represent a DUNA. Multiple allowed; they can enter and leave a chat, have different handles, be called for different purposes.
  • Personal Allies (member allies) — represent a member.
  • Alliances — a container within a DUNA grouping any allies (Personal, DUNA, Program, even other Alliances). Temporary or long-term; no legal standing of its own; its root/cover is the DUNA where it was initiated.
  • Sponsors — allies that represent/sell offerings.
  • Programs — built from Enact: a Program plus the Inform/Instruct/Empower that turn it into an Ally.

Who can create what (gated by level)

  • Member: Personal Allies (unlimited) and Alliances.
  • Founder: everything a Member can, plus DUNA Allies (for DUNAs they founded).
  • Builder: everything a Founder can, plus Programs.
  • Sponsor: everything above, plus offering/Sponsor allies.

20. Rationale for design calls

A few places where I moved beyond a literal reading of the brief, and why.

The account button is the level badge

The brief asked to move the button between the profile and the bell away from Earn toward account management. The element sitting there is the level badge. Making the badge itself the entry to role management reads naturally: you tap your standing to change your standing. Earn (the wallet ledger) stays in the Active rail, and "Reload" there deep-links to Load Wallet, so nothing is lost.

One Account screen, two modes

Buy-Coins (first run, Stripe) and Manage-role (logged-in, Load Wallet) are the same screen with the payment area swapped. It keeps the tier boxes identical so the mental model "your role is the Coins you hold" is taught once and reused. The reader-app note appears in both when the environment is mobile-installed.

The contextual dock collapses rather than competes

An always-open right rail is generous on a wide monitor and oppressive on a phone. On desktop it pushes content; below 1100px it overlays as a sheet and falls back to a single tab/button. The DUNA Ally is the same entity you would chat with full-screen, so the dock is a shortcut, not a second system.

Locked sections stay visible

Rather than hide what a Guest or Member cannot yet do, the navigation shows the locked items with a small lock and a "hold more Coins" nudge. It teaches the ladder and gives the upgrade a reason. If this reads as clutter at the lowest tiers, the fallback is to hide locked items and surface them only on the account page.

Coins, not tokens; and a real price

"Coins" everywhere avoids collision with LLM tokens. I anchored WVDUNA at $0.10 (matching the earlier "$1,240 ≈ 12,400 WVDUNA" figure) so the Coin counts on the buy screen are concrete, and set Mountain Mesh and WV Commerce Club at 1/3 and 2/3 of that with half/double join prices, so engineering can see the math flow through the boxes.

Chat context on DUNA switch

Switching DUNA re-themes everything and updates the contextual dock to that DUNA's Ally. The main Chat list is treated as the member's personal, cross-DUNA conversations, so it does not reset on switch. If you would rather the main thread also follow the DUNA, that is a small change and worth deciding (see §21).
